1. Falling Shoulders (Shoulder Drop)

Shoulder slipping is the #1 complaint with deep back neck blouses. When you take two steps, the blouse shoulder slides down your arm.

Why It Happens:

1. The back neckline width exceeds your shoulder bone span (acromion to acromion).
2. The shoulder slope was cut flat (180°) instead of angled down 1/2 inch to match your body's natural anatomical slope.
3. The armhole curve was cut too deep, causing arm motion to pull the shoulder strap downward.

The Tailoring Fix:

Immediate Alteration: Unpick the shoulder seam and slant the stitch 1/4 to 1/2 inch deeper at the neck end while keeping the sleeve end untouched. If the neck is exceptionally wide, add handcrafted fabric dori tassels or install lingerie shoulder snaps that lock around your bra straps.

Prevention: When placing an order, review our 12-point blouse measurement guide to record accurate across-shoulder span.

2. Tight Armholes & Underarm Pinching

A tight armhole leaves red marks, restricts arm movement, and causes heavy sweating. This is especially painful with stiff metallic wire on hand zardozi blouses.

Why It Happens:

The pattern master cut the armhole depth (scye depth) based on generic bust size rather than measuring the exact arm circumference at the armpit fold.

The Tailoring Fix:

Carefully unpick the side seam from the armpit down 2 inches. Scoop out 3/8 inch of fabric from the lowest curve of the armhole scye. Let out 1/2 inch of the inside seam allowance. At Sree Designers, we always build a generous 2.5-inch side margin into every stitched blouse to allow effortless resizing.

3. Back Neck Gaping & Puckering

When you stand upright, does the back neckline float away from your spine instead of sitting flush against your skin?

Why It Happens:

In deep U-neck and keyhole blouses, the back panel has less structural support. If the back waist darts are too short or improperly angled, excess fabric bubbles outward.

The Tailoring Fix:

Deepen the two vertical back waist darts by 1/4 inch each, or take up 1/2 inch at the center lower waistband. For brides who prefer open-back cuts, explore our trending back neck designs guide which explains how canvas interlining prevents gaping.

4. Diagonal Creases Across the Bust (Apex Misalignment)

Diagonal strain lines pointing from the armhole towards the bust apex indicate that the cup volume or dart placement is incorrect.

The Fix: Anatomical Padded Cup Integration

Standard blouses rely on external bras that shift throughout the day. At Sree Designers, our signature solution is built-in multi-density contoured cups stitched directly between the outer silk and high-thread-count cotton lining. This eliminates bra lines, provides natural lift, and guarantees zero puckering across the front drape.

5. Hook Placket Gaping & Pulling

When the front or back placket strains and reveals small gaps between hooks, the horizontal chest circumference is too tight by 1 to 1.5 inches.

The Tailoring Fix:

Open the side seams from underarm to waist and let out the required margin equally on both sides. Replace standard metal eyelets with a continuous hand-stitched thread-loop placket for superior tension distribution.

6. Sleeve Twisting & Armhole Diagonal Lines

If your elbow-length sleeves twist when you bend your arm, the sleeve cap height is mismatched with the armhole curve. Modern sleeves require distinct front-armhole hollow curves (scooped 1/2 inch deeper in front) compared to the back armhole. Learn more in our trending bridal sleeve designs guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do my saree blouse shoulders keep falling down?
Shoulder drop occurs when the deep back neckline is cut too wide, the shoulder slope angle is incorrect, or the armhole depth is too deep. A master tailor can fix this by taking in the shoulder seam slightly slanted towards the neck, adding decorative dori tassels, or inserting transparent lingerie shoulder snaps.
Can a tight armhole in an embroidered bridal blouse be altered?
Yes. At Sree Designers, we inspect the side seam allowance. By scooping out 1/4 to 1/2 inch from the lower curve of the armhole (the scye) and letting out the side allowance, we restore full arm mobility without disturbing the outer aari or zardozi embroidery.
How do you fix back neck gaping on a deep neck blouse?
Back neck gaping happens when the back waist dart is misplaced or the back length is too long. The solution is creating a subtle contoured dart near the shoulder blade or taking up excess fabric at the lower waistband.
